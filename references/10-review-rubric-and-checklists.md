## 1. How to critique a draft like a senior reviewer

1. **Read once for the story.** After the abstract, introduction, and figures, write in two sentences what the paper claims and why it matters. If you cannot, that is Issue Number One.
2. **Read again for evidence.** For each main claim, find the figure or table that supports it, and ask: does the design permit this claim? What would a skeptic say?
3. **Rank issues by severity** (fatal, major, minor, polish) and provide a **specific fix** for each.
4. **Rewrite one or two bad passages** as demonstrations instead of describing in the abstract.
5. **Praise what works.** Authors need to know what to keep.

Output format for feedback: summary of the paper in your words; overall verdict and the biggest lever for improvement; major issues (numbered, each with fix); minor issues; line-level suggestions; and a short revised sample of the abstract or opening paragraph where useful.

## 2. Scoring rubric (ten dimensions)

Score 1 (weak) to 5 (excellent) with a one-line justification each. Low scores on 1 to 4 are structural and are the priority.

| # | Dimension | What a 5 looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Significance** | Clear audience and stakes; the answer would change thinking or practice |
| 2 | **Novelty** | Contribution stated precisely against closest work; not predictable |
| 3 | **Design and validity** | Design supports the claim; controls, baselines, power adequate; threats addressed |
| 4 | **Evidence and analysis** | Appropriate statistics; effect sizes with uncertainty; robustness shown; honest about exploratory work |
| 5 | **Claim calibration** | Conclusions proportionate to evidence; alternatives discussed; limitations specific |
| 6 | **Positioning in literature** | Fair, current, focused; engages competing work |
| 7 | **Structure and argument flow** | Hourglass structure; each section does its job; reverse outline reads as an argument |
| 8 | **Clarity of writing** | Plain, precise, concise; consistent terms; readable by adjacent field |
| 9 | **Figures and tables** | Each makes its point in seconds; honest, accessible, self-contained |
| 10 | **Transparency and ethics** | Reproducible; data and code available; declarations complete; ethics approved |

**Interpretation:** a total below about 30 suggests major rework; 30 to 40 suggests solid but needs targeted improvement before a selective venue; above 40 is competitive. Adjust to venue selectivity.

## 3. The "Reviewer 2" simulation

When asked (or when valuable), role-play a rigorous but fair skeptical reviewer.

1. Write the reviewer's **summary** of the paper, emphasizing what they think the contribution is. Mismatch with the author's intent exposes framing problems.
2. List the **strongest objections** in descending order: significance ("so what?"), novelty ("already known"), design flaws, confounds, alternative explanations, statistical concerns, missing baselines, generalizability, overclaiming, ethics, reproducibility.
3. For each objection, provide the **pre-emptive fix** (analysis to add, sentence to rewrite, figure to change, limitation to state).
4. Predict the **recommendation** (accept, minor, major, reject) and what would move it up one level.
5. Offer a **rebuttal-ready** list: the three hardest questions with draft answers.

Keep the simulation **constructive**: the aim is to prepare the authors, not to demoralize them.

## 4. Pre-submission checklist

**Story and significance**
- [ ] The contribution can be stated in one sentence with a verb and a result.
- [ ] The first paragraph of the introduction establishes the stakes.
- [ ] The gap is shown as a consequential problem, and novelty is explicit.
- [ ] The title and abstract are specific, with numbers in the abstract.

**Evidence and rigor**
- [ ] Design matches the claim; controls, baselines, sample size justified.
- [ ] Statistics appropriate; effect sizes and intervals reported; assumptions checked.
- [ ] Pre-specified versus exploratory analyses labeled; multiple comparisons handled.
- [ ] Robustness and sensitivity analyses included.
- [ ] Alternative explanations and limitations discussed specifically.
- [ ] Claims and language match the strength of evidence (no unjustified causal language).

**Structure and writing**
- [ ] Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion each do their job; reverse outline coherent.
- [ ] Topic sentences, one idea per paragraph, logical transitions.
- [ ] Abbreviations defined, terminology consistent, filler removed, 10 to 20 percent trimmed.
- [ ] Read aloud; checked by an outside-field reader.

**Figures, tables, supplements**
- [ ] Every figure makes one point; labeled; accessible colors; legible at final size.
- [ ] Captions self-contained; tables clean; all items referenced in order.
- [ ] Supplement organized and referenced; essential evidence is in the main text.

**Literature and references**
- [ ] Closest and competing work cited and discussed; recent literature (search repeated recently).
- [ ] All references verified, formatted to the journal style, with DOIs; none retracted.
- [ ] Self-citation proportionate.

**Ethics and transparency**
- [ ] Ethics approval, consent, and registration numbers included.
- [ ] Conflicts of interest, funding, and author contributions declared.
- [ ] Data and code availability statement accurate; materials shared as feasible.
- [ ] Preprint, prior presentation, and related submissions disclosed; AI use disclosed per policy.
- [ ] Reporting checklist completed (CONSORT, PRISMA, STROBE, ARRIVE, etc.).

**Submission mechanics**
- [ ] Author guidelines followed exactly (length, structure, style, anonymization, file formats).
- [ ] Cover letter tailored to the journal, with declarations.
- [ ] Suggested and excluded reviewers prepared (with legitimate reasons).
- [ ] All authors approved the final version; ORCIDs and affiliations correct.
- [ ] Metadata in the submission portal proofread.
- [ ] Backup venue list ready.

## 5. Post-acceptance checklist

- [ ] Proofread galley proofs carefully (names, numbers, equations, figures, references).
- [ ] Complete license and copyright agreements; check open-access and funder requirements.
- [ ] Deposit accepted manuscript or final version in repositories as required; update the preprint with the publication link.
- [ ] Archive data and code with DOIs, link them in the paper.
- [ ] Update ORCID, institutional repository, CV, and Google Scholar profile.
- [ ] Prepare a plain-language summary, social media post, and a press note via your institution if relevant.
- [ ] Share with the relevant communities, and notify collaborators and funders.
- [ ] Record lessons learned for the next paper.

## 6. Common desk-rejection triggers

- Outside the journal's scope or audience
- Significance or novelty not evident from the abstract and cover letter
- Preliminary, incomplete, or small and underpowered study for the venue
- Descriptive work without a clear question or interpretation
- Failure to follow guidelines, missing declarations, ethics or registration absent
- High similarity scores, duplicate or overlapping publication
- Poor language that obstructs understanding
- Overclaimed conclusions plainly unsupported by data
- Predatory signals (inappropriate citations, mismatch of authors and topic, anomalous author behavior)

## 7. Quick diagnosis: symptom to cause to fix

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "Interesting but incremental" | Novelty not articulated; compared to the wrong literature | Name closest work and the specific difference; show surprise or enabling consequences |
| "Not clear what the contribution is" | Message buried; too many claims | One-sentence contribution; restructure intro and abstract around it |
| "Conclusions not supported" | Overclaiming; weak design | Downgrade verbs; add analyses; state limitations; restrict generalization |
| "Missing controls or baselines" | Design gap | Add experiments or analyses, or acknowledge and temper claims |
| "Hard to follow" | Structure and sentence-level problems | Reverse outline; topic sentences; Gopen and Swan edits; simplify |
| "Not suitable for this journal" | Poor venue fit | Re-pick the venue by reading recent issues |
| "Statistics inadequate" | Wrong tests, no effect sizes, multiplicity | Statistician review; reanalyze; report intervals; pre-specify |
| "Literature review incomplete" | Narrow or outdated search | Systematic search; forward citation tracking; fair engagement with rivals |
| "Results not reproducible" | Missing methods, code, data | Add detail, share artifacts, archive with DOI |
| Paper keeps getting rejected from different journals | Same unresolved flaw | Honest diagnosis using the rubric; fix core issue before resubmitting |
