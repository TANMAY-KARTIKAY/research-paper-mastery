## 1. Contribution statement (write this first)

"We show that [specific finding with direction/magnitude] in [population/system], using [approach], which [matters because consequence], unlike [closest prior work] which [limitation]."

Test: Does it contain a verb, a result, and a reason to care? If not, keep refining.

## 2. Abstract formulas

**Five-sentence core (unstructured):**
1. [Context and why it matters.]
2. [Gap or unresolved problem.]
3. [What we did: design, data, n, method.]
4. [Key results with numbers: effect, interval, accuracy.]
5. [Implication, scaled to the evidence.]

**Structured (biomedical style):**
- **Background:** [one to two sentences].
- **Objective:** [one sentence].
- **Methods:** [design, setting, participants, interventions or exposures, outcomes, analysis].
- **Results:** [n; primary outcome with effect size and 95% CI; key secondary results].
- **Conclusions:** [what it means, with the main limitation].

**CS/ML style:** problem, why existing approaches fall short, our approach in one clause, headline results versus strongest baselines (numbers), and availability (code or data).

## 3. Introduction skeleton

- **P1 (stakes):** [Broad problem and why it matters, concretely.]
- **P2 to P3 (known):** [Synthesized prior work moving general to specific; cite for claims.]
- **P4 (gap):** "However, [what is unknown or contested], which limits [consequence]."
- **P5 (this work):** "Here we [approach] to test [question]. We find [main result]. This [implication]."
- **Optional P6:** [Roadmap in one sentence.]

## 4. Cover letter

```
[Date]

Dear [Dr./Prof. Editor Surname / Dear Editor],

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, "[Title]", for consideration as a [article type] in [Journal].

[Question and gap, 1 to 2 sentences: Despite X, it remains unknown whether Y, which matters because Z.]

[What we did and found, 2 sentences: Using [approach] in [sample/system], we found [main result with a key number].]

[Why this journal, 1 to 2 sentences: Because [journal] readers are interested in [topic], and recent papers such as [verified related paper in the journal] address [related theme], we believe this work will interest your audience because [specific reason].]

This manuscript is original, has not been published, and is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors have approved the submission. [Ethics approval/registration: ...] [Conflicts of interest and funding: ...] [Related preprint/related submission: ...] [Data and code availability: ...]

[Optional: We suggest the following reviewers, who have no conflicts of interest: Name, institution, email, expertise. We ask that [name] not be asked to review because [legitimate reason].]

Thank you for your time and consideration.

Sincerely,
[Corresponding author name, title, affiliation, email, ORCID]
On behalf of all authors
```

Keep to one page. No hype, no abstract copy-paste.

## 5. Pre-submission enquiry

```
Dear Dr. [Editor],

I am writing to ask whether [Journal] would be interested in a manuscript on [topic].

[Abstract in 150 to 200 words.]

[Two sentences on why it fits the journal.]

I would be glad to provide the full manuscript. Thank you for your consideration.

[Name, affiliation]
```

## 6. Response to reviewers letter

```
Manuscript ID: [ID]
Title: [Title]

Dear Dr. [Editor],

Thank you for the opportunity to revise our manuscript and for the constructive comments from the reviewers. We have addressed all points. The main changes are:

1. [Major change 1, e.g., added analysis X (new Fig. 3)].
2. [Major change 2, e.g., clarified the Methods on Y (page 6, lines 120 to 140)].
3. [Major change 3, e.g., expanded the Discussion on limitations (page 12)].

Our point-by-point responses follow. Reviewer comments are in italics; our responses are in plain text; changes in the manuscript are highlighted in yellow in the tracked version.

---
REVIEWER 1

Comment 1.1: "[Paste the comment verbatim.]"

Response: We thank the reviewer for this point. [Agree/clarify/explain what you did.] [Evidence or new analysis.] We have revised the manuscript accordingly (Page X, lines Y to Z): "[Quote the revised text.]"

Comment 1.2: "[...]"

Response: We appreciate this suggestion. [If you respectfully disagree:] We have considered this carefully. However, [reason with evidence/citation]. To address the underlying concern, we now [what you changed], and we state this limitation explicitly (Page X, lines Y to Z).

---
REVIEWER 2
[Repeat.]

---
Additional changes: [e.g., updated references, corrected typos, aligned numbers after reanalysis.]

We hope the revised manuscript is now suitable for publication in [Journal] and look forward to your decision.

Sincerely,
[Names]
```

**Rules of thumb:** thank once, not repeatedly; be specific about where changes are; never ignore a comment; address the concern behind a misunderstanding by clarifying the text; keep tone neutral and professional.

## 7. Rebuttal for conference reviews (short window)

```
We thank all reviewers. Key points:

**Common concern A: [e.g., missing baseline]** We added [baseline] and report [result] ([Table X in rebuttal/appendix]); our method still [outcome].

**Common concern B: [e.g., novelty]** [Concise difference vs. closest work, 2 sentences.]

**R1:** 
- Q1 [paraphrase]: [direct answer + evidence].
- Q2 ...

**R2:** ...

We will incorporate these changes in the camera-ready version.
```

Prioritize the concerns driving low scores; answer directly and briefly; add only results you can truthfully produce; avoid tone that attacks reviewers.

## 8. Reviewer report skeleton

1. **Summary of the submission** (3 to 5 sentences: question, approach, claims).
2. **Assessment:** strengths (significance, novelty, rigor) and the principal weaknesses.
3. **Major concerns** (numbered): the issue, why it matters for the conclusion, and a suggested analysis or revision.
4. **Minor concerns** (numbered): clarity, presentation, references, typos.
5. **Ethics, data, and reporting:** any issues.
6. **Recommendation** and rationale; **confidential note to the editor** if needed.

## 10. Reviewer-suggestion note

"[Name], [Institution], [email]. Expertise: [topic]. Rationale: published on [specific relevant subtopic]. No collaboration, co-authorship, or institutional ties with the authors in the past [3 to 5] years."

## 11. Status enquiry and appeal letters

**Status enquiry (after well beyond the stated median):**
"Dear Editorial Office, I am writing to ask about the status of manuscript [ID], submitted on [date]. We understand reviewer availability varies and would be grateful for any update. Thank you."

**Appeal (only for clear errors):**
"Dear Dr. [Editor], Thank you for considering our manuscript [ID]. We respectfully request reconsideration because [specific factual or procedural error], as detailed below: [point 1 with evidence], [point 2 with evidence]. We would be happy to [provide additional analysis / suggest an alternative reviewer]. We understand that the decision rests with you."

## 12. Limitations paragraph pattern

"Our study has limitations. First, [limitation 1: design/sample], which may [specific threat]; we addressed this by [mitigation], and [what it does and does not imply for our conclusion]. Second, [limitation 2]. Third, [limitation 3]. These limitations suggest that [claim scope], and future work should [concrete study]. Despite them, [the robust finding that survives]."

Be specific, state the direction of likely bias where known, and avoid generic boilerplate ("small sample size").
