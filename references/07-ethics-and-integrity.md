## 1. Why integrity is a publication issue

Journals screen for integrity with similarity software, image forensics, statistical consistency checks, and registration checks. A reputation, once damaged by retraction or an investigation, takes years to rebuild. Most integrity problems begin as small shortcuts under deadline pressure. Make the honest option the easy one by planning early.

## 2. Research ethics approval and consent

- Research involving **human participants, identifiable data, or animals** requires approval from an **institutional review board or ethics committee**, obtained **before** data collection. Retroactive approval is generally not accepted.
- State the **approval body, protocol number, and date**, and the **consent procedure** (written, verbal, waived and why). For minors, vulnerable groups, or sensitive topics, describe added safeguards.
- Follow the **Declaration of Helsinki** for medical research, the **Belmont principles** (respect for persons, beneficence, justice), and field codes (APA, BPS, ASA, etc.). Animal studies follow **ARRIVE** and the 3Rs (replacement, reduction, refinement).
- Protect **privacy and confidentiality**: de-identify data, store securely, avoid identifying details in case reports or quotations. Follow GDPR, HIPAA, or the local data protection regime as applicable.
- For secondary data analysis, state the data source's terms and whether further ethics review was required or waived.
- Field research in other countries: respect local approvals, community consent, and equitable collaboration, including local co-authorship and benefit sharing where appropriate.
- **Dual-use research** and sensitive topics (biosecurity, security vulnerabilities, personal data): consider the risks of publishing and follow institutional and journal guidance.

## 3. Authorship integrity

- Use **ICMJE criteria** (or the field's equivalent): substantial contribution, drafting or critical revision, final approval, and accountability. All authors must be able to take responsibility for the paper's content.
- **No gift, guest, or ghost authorship.** Do not add senior names to boost acceptance. Do not omit those who earned authorship, including junior researchers and students.
- Agree on the **author list and order** in writing early; document contributions with **CRediT**.
- Changes to the author list after submission require **written consent from all authors** and journal approval.
- **AI tools do not qualify as authors** because they cannot take responsibility.
- Disputes: use institutional research integrity officers, ombudspersons, or COPE guidance. Do not submit while a dispute is unresolved.

## 4. Plagiarism, text recycling, and duplicate publication

- **Plagiarism** includes copying text, ideas, data, or figures without attribution, and close paraphrase that stays too near the source's structure and wording. Cite and quote properly.
- **Self-plagiarism and text recycling:** reusing your own published text or figures without citation and permission. Reusing short, standard Methods descriptions is sometimes tolerated if cited and disclosed, but follow journal policy. Check copyright in your earlier publications.
- **Duplicate (redundant) publication:** the same findings published twice. Do not submit the same manuscript to several journals simultaneously. Disclose any related manuscripts, preprints, theses, and conference papers in the cover letter.
- **Salami slicing:** splitting one study into several minimal papers to inflate output. Publish one coherent story; if multiple papers are justified, cross-reference and disclose.
- Journals commonly run similarity checks (e.g., iThenticate). A high overlap score needs explanation; fix by rewriting and citing, not by evasion tricks such as synonym swapping tools.
- A thesis or dissertation is usually acceptable prior material, but state it.

## 5. Data fabrication, falsification, and image integrity

- **Fabrication** (making up data) and **falsification** (manipulating data, processes, images, or results) are the gravest misconduct. Never do them, and never "fill in" missing values by invention.
- **Selective reporting** and **p-hacking** (testing many ways and reporting only what works) are questionable research practices that undermine reliability. Report all analyses, outcomes, and exclusions.
- **Image integrity:** no splicing, duplication, or selective alteration; apply adjustments uniformly and disclose; keep originals and raw data.
- **Raw data retention:** keep raw data, code, and lab notebooks for the period your institution and funders require (often several years) so any query can be answered.
- Statistical forensic tools can detect impossible summary statistics and digit patterns. Accurate record-keeping protects you.

## 6. Conflicts of interest and funding

- Declare **financial** (employment, consulting, equity, patents, grants, honoraria) and **non-financial** (personal, political, competitive, intellectual) interests that could reasonably be seen as influencing the work.
- State **funder names and grant numbers** and whether funders had any role in design, analysis, interpretation, or the decision to publish.
- Industry-sponsored work should state who analyzed data and who controlled publication.
- Disclose relationships with suggested reviewers; do not suggest friends, collaborators, or same-institution colleagues.
- If in doubt, **disclose**. Undisclosed conflicts discovered later are far worse than disclosed ones.

## 7. Data, code, and privacy

- Share data and code **unless there is a legitimate reason** (privacy, legal, commercial, security), and say so in the availability statement with access conditions.
- Use **trusted repositories** with persistent identifiers (DOIs) and licenses.
- Data from human participants must be shared only within the **consent terms**; de-identify and consider re-identification risk (small cells, rare conditions, geolocation).
- Follow **Indigenous data sovereignty** principles (CARE principles) where relevant.
- Respect **third-party licenses and terms of use** for datasets, code, and scraped data; scraping may violate terms or privacy rules.
- Document **data provenance** and any exclusions or transformations.

## 8. Preprints, prior presentation, and scooping

- Preprints are permitted by most journals; **verify the target journal's policy** and disclose the preprint in the cover letter.
- Presenting at conferences is generally fine; disclose it and check conference-proceedings policies regarding the overlap with a journal paper.
- **Priority and scooping:** posting a preprint timestamps your work. If you discover a closely related paper, cite it, differentiate your contribution honestly, and consider adjusting your claims. Do not hide it.
- **Embargoes and press:** some journals restrict media coverage before publication (e.g., Ingelfinger-style rules in some medical journals). Check policy before releasing press material.

## 9. AI tools in research and writing

- **Follow the target journal's and publisher's AI policy**, which typically requires: disclosure of generative AI use in writing or analysis (often in Methods or Acknowledgments), no AI authorship, and **authors' full responsibility** for the content, accuracy, and originality.
- **Never trust AI-generated references, quotes, or facts without verification.** AI tools can produce plausible but non-existent citations and subtly wrong claims.
- Do not use AI to **fabricate data, images, or results**. Image generation for scientific figures is typically prohibited unless it is the subject of the research and disclosed.
- **Confidentiality:** do not upload unpublished manuscripts of others (peer review), sensitive participant data, or proprietary data into tools that may retain or train on them, unless approved.
- Acceptable and common uses (check policy): language editing, brainstorming, structuring, code assistance, summarizing public literature, with human verification. Keep a record of what was used and how.
- The researcher remains **accountable** for every sentence, number, and citation.

## 10. Corrections, retractions, and handling errors

- If you find an error after submission or publication, **tell the editor promptly**. Honest errors lead to **corrections (errata)**; serious ones that invalidate conclusions lead to **retraction**, which, when initiated by the authors for honest error, is a mark of integrity rather than shame.
- Cooperate fully with investigations; preserve data and communications.
- Consult **COPE flowcharts** and your institution's research integrity office.
- Retraction Watch Database and publisher notices help check whether sources you cite were retracted.

## 11. Misconduct red flags and where to turn

Red flags in your own project: pressure to get a result, unwillingness to share raw data, data that look too clean, undocumented exclusions, "fixing" results after the fact, uncomfortable authorship demands, unexplained image similarities, or reluctance to let others see the analysis.

If you see concerns in a colleague's or supervisor's work: document factually, consult a trusted mentor, and use institutional channels (research integrity officer, ombudsperson, whistleblower protection). Journals and COPE also provide guidance. Raise concerns about published papers with the journal editor, or via PubPeer for open discussion.

## 12. Integrity checklist

- [ ] Ethics approval obtained before data collection and cited with number.
- [ ] Consent procedures and data protection described.
- [ ] Trial or review registered if required.
- [ ] All authors meet criteria; contributions agreed; order agreed in writing.
- [ ] Conflicts of interest and funding disclosed for all authors.
- [ ] Prior publications, preprints, and related submissions disclosed.
- [ ] Text, data, and images are original or properly attributed and permitted.
- [ ] Images unaltered beyond disclosed, uniform adjustments; originals retained.
- [ ] All analyses, outcomes, and exclusions reported; exploratory work labeled.
- [ ] Data and code shared or restrictions explained.
- [ ] AI-use disclosed per policy; every reference verified.
- [ ] Submitting to one journal only at a time.
